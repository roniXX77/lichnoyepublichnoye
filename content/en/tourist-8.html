Almaty How do you learn to fly? How do you manage to take a running start so hard-hard, so sharp-sharp, and jump down with such a ring that the little pane in the porthole cracks? How do I undo the little buttons, how do I throw off the harness of text – bunches of waste? How the fuck do you learn to fly again? How do you find the quiet of tenderness? We’re quiet? How’s that? How do you ask questions the right way, so it’s neither yes nor no, so i love you, but i’m flying away, flying away, but sealed in industrial metal, running, but staying put, no wings – invent a marvel of engineering? A marvel of anti-flight? It’s not even a question, it’s just a collective observation. I run without clothes or skin. My cornea is coming apart in layers. My chest goes pink where it touches the Caspian waves. Passing looks caress my nervous nakedness and make my whole body shiver, vulgar and ticklish. I feel the speed of the wind, I lick her cheeks, earlobes, eyelashes, ribs – and they look at me like I’m sick. Like I’m a shaman. Like I’m a slut. People notice a lot, especially keenly – how somebody runs, but nobody sees how I, naked, tearful, hungry, torn, pick up speed as I run – so I can fly somewhere far ahead, beyond the visible trajectory. And airplane coffee has its own particular suffocating aftertaste of escapism. Alex, you wouldn’t get it, you know, you haven’t seen a movie like this before, you’ll rrrot, decay, shoot yourself, Alex, we’re watching a movie where you die! I don’t want to, really. See, I’m crying? See, now it’s all over for me too. Just a little prick, a little sex, a little death – and we’re done. You don’t cry anymore, because the dead don’t mourn themselves. Come take my hand with your little pieces of ice. We’ll dig one hole for the two of us, you’ll be young, you’ll rot, I’ll dig deeper and deeper – we’ll laugh at trifles, sometimes drink and make toasts, sometimes sit in silence un-lovingly. We’ll know forever that the end has come. We killed ourselves. Urban gatherers collect things, films, fragments, shards – everything except anything useful. We’re collected by little slips of paper, petty officials, close ones, family, exes, little widows, little people, little graveyards, we get strung like beads, get as annoying as a piece of shot caught in your teeth. The little blue ball in my palm looks like a little glass eye that’s gathered every Caspian tear inside it. I carry it carefully past Almaty’s angular bits, past unreal mountains and last year’s fountains. When I was in Tarusa, we loved playing with balls like this: tossing them between floors in our little house, racing each other at tag on the village asphalt, messing around with kisses in between. Ket, ket, ket. Shoo, that is. I still can’t stop thinking of the evening we came back from Tarusa. I was very beautiful and smoking a cigarette, and a thought flickered: «What if this is our last evening like this?» I didn’t know yet that there was a «ket» for times like that, but in my heart I kept repeating it, chasing away the thought, chasing myself away until I caught up with myself – in the reflection of the little blue ball, a model of the unreal Earth of my dreams for a hundred tenge. I hid the little eye in my palm. How do you hold the whole planet, the entire world inside yourself so the waves don’t carry it far, far away into weightlessness? Squeeze your eyes shut. Ket, tears, ket. You’re so colorless, you belong in the glints on Soviet high-rises, yes, right at the top, in the reflection of the mountains, in stone, in solidity. The child’s heart crashed: the little ball doesn’t bounce anymore; it turns blue, grows mold in my palm, wastes away, holds the entire planet inside itself – or three months, to be exact. There are often earthquakes in Almaty, it sits on seven active plates, a friend told me over tea this evening. Then all the buildings could be leveled to nothing. Even the oldest and strongest high-rises. Then the ball will bounce again, the tears will spill again, blind rain will come, and my little eyes will flood our long-suffering Earth again. Norah Jones is playing in the kitchen, coffee’s getting cold. I don’t feel like going anywhere, I’m too lazy even to smoke a cigarette by the entrance and throw the butt in the empty «Jacobs Monarch» jar by the courtyard bench. Where’s there to go when the city isn’t yours and it’s so quiet, so unassuming? It troubles me. I always thought I was thoroughly warped and trembling because I was from Moscow, that what I needed was a vacation and everything would write itself. But the Kazakh greenery and quiet of the well-off neighborhoods stirs anxiety in me: not enough shaking, not enough seismic activity, too much, too much, calm as I am I torment myself. The sound of my keyboard calms me here. The metro lulls me, its lack of hurry plunging me into cosmic weightlessness. Also – the never-ending jazz track in Zhenya’s kitchen, and me, with no idea when it (the kitchen) will end or if it will. But where is the end point in all this? These are entirely nomadic things, so much so there’s no point even describing them. They just exist, flow, shimmer in flashes in my tired timeless head, they just move like microbes, and watching them move I’ve frozen on the couch with my bare legs folded above eye level. Scratch the curly crown of my head, make out the Eiffel Tower on the old wallpaper through my short fingers – maybe brew some more Rwanda – it would be good to break through this dense mass of laziness and calm, smash it – only it’s non-Newtonian and flows like jelly, it just bounces back peaceably and won’t let me out of the cocoon of pleasant sufractal weakness.
