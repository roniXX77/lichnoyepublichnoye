So much love. People love me, the city loves me – what? If not now, then when – is it time? If not when the sweet juice of watermelon sinks into your gums while we watch the coat scene in «Courier» toward morning; if not when I still haven’t learned the poem in the postcard by heart, but have already read it to myself a few times with a little tenderness; if not when drawings on skin can be many-colored, and September sun – white; if not when people in Novokosino walk along the solid white line while at Chistye they dismantle artists’ studios, and if – to walk past the Pyaterochka in Tarusa from another life, barefoot through the dust, and laugh – «what’s next?» Next was over long ago, today has come. *** When it still seemed like the move was a long way off, my teacher said to me: «Take the food you’ll miss most with you to America». I believed her and started thinking about what I’d miss. Would I miss fake fast food, the way we snuck into an opening on Pushkinskaya happy as children, a point-and-shoot in our hands? Or maybe Iranian Coca-Cola? What are you supposed to miss in a wonderful country that doesn’t exist? Shyly: «And a Hematogen bar, please, Mom, ask for Hematogen». When Mom went to the pharmacy, every time she bought me a different brand or a new flavor, and I always wondered how she managed it – Hematogen doesn’t come in different flavors, it isn’t even food so how could it have a taste. But the best one was still the yellow one with the little hedgehog, for forty rubles. In Yekaterinburg we bought about five of those, probably – though I may be embellishing, of course – we were seventeen, the air went to our heads. We needed condoms, and we walked around Uralmash pretending to be embarrassed. We went into every pharmacy we passed. «Buying Hematogen is an intimate business», – I reminded myself yesterday as I went into the pharmacy on Komsomolskaya for another tube of strong painkillers. You can’t switch the pain off when you’re going out of town to forget tomorrow’s flight. You can’t switch the pain off when something taut, hot and moaning has moved into your belly. «And a Hematogen bar, please», – to myself – «when did non-food get so expensive?» i’m like a film that never got made. «Are you flying alone?» So rude, hurtful, squeaky. While I’m boarding a flight to Los Angeles somewhere in a country with a made-up name, in the middle of the abyss, Moscow consumption starts to wear off. I look at the black-and-white curls in my passport and the tense forehead, festively overhead – UNITED STATES OF AMERICA – and a long-forgotten feeling rises inside. Déjà vu in the future declension. The knowledge that whatever happens after I step over the threshold of the Qatari plane has already happened somewhere, sometime. Weathering. Bleaching. A big rusty elevator shaft you fall through endlessly, realizing something new and big is starting, something called MY LIFE (festively overhead – UNITED STATES OF AMERICA), but nobody’s told me about it yet. So I have to pretend the bleaching isn’t noticeable yet. That it’s decoloring rather than bleaching. I’m filmed in shards like a bad movie: i eat a runny cream dessert on the plane, feel the metallic taste of the spoon on my tongue, hear American speech this close for the first time, stand up – i’m on the aisle – stand up again, the melatonin glitches, in my pocket – a little paper medal that says «Pool Shark» (in small print: sun echoing through the empty chambers of the heart). A film snapshot like this, blurry and airless, will be my first immigrant photograph. An American who’s too Russian. With honors stuffed into her pockets, and paper honors at that. With a shirt forever wrinkled but obligatory. With hair straightened out by the emptiness. My heart’s dried out from the plastic airplane smell. Yes, Moscow consumption is wearing off. Somewhere in a thin trickle California fever is beginning. I met the tuner in a Petersburg February. No, that’s not it. February doesn’t exist in California. It’s hard for someone who, like me, is an Aquarius, celebrates snow, fires a salute to bitterness and lives crusted over with snow to believe that. Still, it’s the absolute truth. My tongue breaks. I forget Russian like a terrible nightmare, and I’m ashamed of it. I know how to casually make small talk and pretend I’m amused and curious enough. My fingers have stiffened and burned, I don’t think with them anymore, just as I don’t think in Russian rhymes, chords, oddities. But there’s one thing I can say for sure: February doesn’t exist in California, just plastic Russian. This is just our Moscow manifesto, yours and mine.
