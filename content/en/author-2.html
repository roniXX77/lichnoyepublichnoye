A lie – a vice, a sign that someone is suffering – destroys trust. Simple so far. But how many lies does it take to destroy it completely? Is the effect cumulative or does it take "just the first time"? Maybe the second? You don’t have to be afraid people will hurt you or damage your shell, because they’re capable of it a priori, and so it’s in their nature rather than a vice specific to certain situations, and you have to accept nature regardless of its connotations instead of rejecting and fearing it. There’s no need to fear the world around you. I’m not afraid of the world around me, I embrace it and learn to accept it, like a researcher or a sister or a daughter. We simply exist – and that’ s the hardest thing You have to accept everything the way it is. A message — is a message, plates — are plates, men — are men. And life — is life. Practice 1. Sometimes trust in a person falls apart even when you understand your fear of material reality, as described above. When someone comes close, I seize them with my tentacles, leave slime on them, traces, kisses. Gradually I separate them from the conscious reality around me, which I don’t fear. It’s as though I swallow them the way an oyster swallows a grain of sand, and begin building a little world around them inside me, with no point of contact with the world outside. The person stops being part of the reality I’m not afraid of. Instead, they can very easily hurt my insides if I don’t wrap them in a little mother-of-pearl ball quickly enough. If something invasive happens while I’m framing and building an inner reality around this person – for example, a Lie, or better yet, an Untruth – the fragile world inside is destroyed at once. First, because it isn’t real, and destroying an Illusion – is very easy. Second, because it’s small and inside. However hard you try, everything that happens in reality – is nature. It can’t cause fear or suffering, because it is the way it is, and you can only observe it – you can’t destroy it. Suffering appears where we confuse the inner world with the outer one, where we start thinking our true reality is the pearls inside our fragile little emotional shells. At this point the person I’m swallowing wounds my soft tissues in one, first, single act, while I can’t grasp how far inside I am, to what extent I’m – a mollusk. I recreate a model of our room out of thin porcelain at a scale of 2 to 1. All the walls, vases, objects are made of it. Gradually I destroy it with my own hands, one thing at a time, until it all collapses. With what? A hammer? A Lie? An Untruth? For now it’s an open question. I’m in a mask, because I can’t wear a truthful face while I destroy it. I’m in character.
