Aktau to run exhaustion in a jumble over the pale knuckles of impressions, to eat into the skin with icy sea salt /i – will be. or – is that the question?/ In a new setting I’m – an [[outsider]]. i’m – a native stranger, a modest observer. i’m – touching this cosmic world i don’t know, timidly, holding my breath waiting to jump /into the depths of ice/ Aktau feels like a city outside time, outside the geographical and semantic conventions of reality. Its metaphysics separates into layers in the air, the smoke of oil plants mixed with sandy desert air, the breath of the sea and the reek of industrial frustration peculiar to little towns like this, all dressed up in market rags and concrete lace. Complete urban dereliction reigns – the distances between buildings seem to stretch for thousands of kilometers, the courtyards look like dried-out pieces of desert in a post-Soviet setting of half-ruined concrete apartment blocks, and every day the label "microdistrict" gets more and more numbers tacked on to mark identical boxes that will never be finished. A city that survived the apocalypse of Soviet reality, recent crises and endless industrialization. "All your Moscow people came out here, then almost all of them left. Nobody likes this place – there’s just oil and the sea here, nothing else" – the sad old taxi driver babbles as I sneak a look at Aktau through neon-tinted windows. It really is empty and cluttered at once – like some giant threw his moving boxes and supermarket bags down in the middle of the steppe. Outside the city the landscape is a mix of noir tales of the East and shots from George Lucas: village houses of sandstone, "windcatchers", the occasional – camel, but mostly – in a very strange way, unfamiliar to a cluttered little Moscow soul – empty. emptiness. /Chapaev never dreamed of it!/ The surrealism of the place lies in its awkwardness and out-of-placeness /pun/. People here are like aliens too, unable to find a place for themselves /take the taxi driver who’s really a bard, or the lost student from Almaty. Well, or me, the lost almost-student from Moscow. Well, or Olya and her little red coat, entirely out of place in the city’s grayness/. Today I walked along the sea cliffs, found inscriptions from 1950 in the porous rock, a monument to Shevchenko /we greeted each other respectfully and parted /ways and byways//, bought a Rubik’s Cube, biting my elbows at my own pointlessness. /well? well!/ The one thing that belongs here – is the sea. It’s smooth and bright-icy, like a paltry artist’s feelings sharpening by December. It’s a little funny how things made by people usually look orderly beside the natural chaos of our unkind little blue ball, but here – it’s the other way round. ...I stood on the shore and looked the sea in the eyes. Only the thin film of my sunglasses separates us. Me and you. You – and me. I’ll stand here for a long, long time looking enviously into your mother-of-pearl soul, sea. You are – the motion of waves and the stillness of frozen shallows. You are – the scarlet blood of algae and the green beauty of living grass /it’s alive, and the city is dead. you – life, us – desert./ You are – pained moans in the songs of gulls, you are – the peaceful happiness of the eastern sun going under. You grow windy. I look into your eyes and – cry. I’ve never cried so quietly before, sea. With your own fresh-water tears, stranger Caspian. /in a whisper/ please... darling, shameless Wash away wash these tears away take them into Yourself, wash all of me away, knock me off my feet, carry me out to the ships. a little more and i’ll take one little step too far on your thin ice and tear, my soul will tear, the way his lace will tear. let me choke – I turn away. And smile – your – watercolor smile. And laugh with dry lungs – your – loose, scattered laugh of water splashing. /I know why I came to you today, why my spaceship from the distant planet Russia accidentally dropped me right at the edge./ It – doesn’t hurt.
