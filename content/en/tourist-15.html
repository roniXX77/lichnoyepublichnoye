i don’t know why or how to begin. we’re already on the ground, but still flying in a plane full of emptiness and time zones smeared across the surface. we’re still not allowed to move. and we still face the prospect of not living to the end of the turbulence. i get on a commuter train to new york. an industrial landscape flies past me. the train gives my life acceleration and direction. my body can’t move on its own. i’ve stopped and now i can only feel time with my smoldering gray skin, as though somebody stuck electrodes all over my surface and wired my nerves to the air without oxygen. i’m wasting away. i’m used to wasting away here. brittle time so brittle the hands of the clock cry tenderly and crunch about something there’s a burn mark left on my skin birthmarks won’t wash off with hunger nobody starts catching fare dodgers i come to new york completely alone for the first time. the concrete walls here make your mouth dry, but with a black coat and a straighter walk – you can time yourself to the celebration, to the dryness and the white noise. i move with texture. the train back gets home at three thirty in the morning. the ticket inspector moves past the seats with texture. they top up my textured plastic cup with martini. the bill in the taxi app hits my card balance with texture. the question «what percentage of ___ seems useless to you» goes strangely flat in the middle of manhattan. after that reality ends because it stops spinning. i go back to my house. it’s gone damp, and the ceiling squeezes my throat. rain pours down the sleek walls somewhere outside. my surface only senses sounds. the sound of water, as if i’d already died, as if i weren’t here anymore. the sound of breakfast, as if everything were all right, as if things got easier for us to understand after chechnya. there’s no end to the state of being a witness. white brains fester, skin cries. the only way to be safe – cancel all concerts and subway trips forever. stop time forever and cork up what’s left within four walls. however quickly i check the dispatches, the news comes lazily, from another time. absence wears you down. i have no right to be so isolated that messages from moscow come late. the plane takes off, seasonally, like the coming of spring. a child screams like at birth. so the plane is alive. and we inside it are transparent and unprotected. there’s a strange crack overhead. quite possibly we’re coming apart. we take off, and either we’re lucky enough to get there or we aren’t. dual cats locked in black boxes, scratching the walls. nobody deserves to know what death they deserve. i won’t deserve to know either if i go out the door right now and drown in a puddle. only that would actually be funny. better not to crawl out of here. i look up at the sky – i spit, i cry – and up there it’s only old plaster. i’m sitting in a box with windows in the ceiling. i was put here for experiments with time. i sit with my face pulled into the onscreen world of terrorism, my eyes molt and fall into my heels, a sign of the greatest nakedness, my body knows its time has gone damp here and only my desperate clear gaze is fixed on the window cracked open, beyond which cars hum and rain drizzles as though time outside us really does flow. i see holes in our struggle one of them swallows me and the thin skin doesn’t tear i turn into an orbital station i become a helpless satellite of this radioactivity i write desperate letters to the bosses in heaven they don’t answer
