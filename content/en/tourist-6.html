An der anderen seit Black [[coffee]] eats away /with a little spoon/ at the whiteness of an airplane cup and my dental composure. Strawberry oil /for lips/ and petroleum jelly /for tattoos/ got mixed up in the bag that holds nothing, with endless boarding passes. Linen shirt on bare skin. I’ve ended up in this place and time again – in a box, a black box defined by miles and a pinching feeling in my heart. One America later I came back to you for a date in economy class. A collar. Linen shirt. New York. American [[commuter trains]]. California joints. Mumiy Troll. I don’t recognize myself. The Serbs don’t recognize me either. That was all that managed to flash through my pink curly head before it was dunked in an endless trip of anger, black ice, sex, vodka, guitars, colored cigarettes and candy wrappers. Scribbling about a trip – is tacky. A writer should always be an observer. A writer should reflect only on the comedown, because it’s on the comedown that observations are laid bare – just like that, uncontrivedly. I press my sweaty-shirted back against a red seat in Istanbul airport – this is where the point of observation begins. Next to me two Turkish girls speak a mix of German and Turkish, to my surprise – in a pseudo-American accent. I press my back against the tender difficulties of translation. I definitely don’t know this [[language]]. I know only a couple of phrases in Turkish, just enough to tell it’s Turkish. No, German after all. Schlaf lecker. But with a taste of Turkish coffee. I doubt anyone needs me here, managing to be a foreigner to every nationality and language, but still getting into everything. Voyeurism – is vulgar, but penetration – is brave. I penetrate without permission.
